01

Solution intent: the customer is the group, not the deal

Scope

This is a commercial credit cockpit, and the customer it opens is the relationship group, not a deal and not a single legal entity.

The customer is the whole connected obligor group: the operating company, its owners and principals, the guarantors, the affiliated companies and subsidiaries, and the household. Exposure, wallet, risk, KYC, and profitability all roll up at the group / obligor grain. A single deal, one nCino Product Package, is one facet of that customer, a drill-in, never the center. A relationship can carry several packages over time; the customer outlives any one of them.

The verdict, at the group grain

The cockpit is a verdict, not a viewer. It opens a relationship group and says, in one banker sentence, whether the customer is healthy, how large the whole exposure is across the group, what the single thing to worry about is, and the one best move to make right now. It does not hand the banker a grid of exposure, rating, and wallet fields and leave them to do the reading.

The governing rule for every element on screen: if you would render a table, render the conclusion the table implies plus the drill to the detail for anyone who wants to audit it. The table is the thing you drill to, never the thing you are handed and asked to interpret.

Commercial credit is the spine. KYC, collateral monitoring, and covenant monitoring are first-class through-lines that run the length of the relationship. Deposits and treasury wallet, portfolio context, and profitability are supporting relationship context at the group grain, not the organizing axis.

What it is not

  • Not an autonomous decider. It concludes, drafts, and routes; a human commits every credit action.
  • Not a place where regulated numbers are guessed. Exposure roll-ups, covenant grades, collateral coverage, and routing verdicts are deterministic server-side code reading verified fields.
  • Not a sharing bypass. It runs as the authenticated user, so Salesforce sharing and FLS are the floor on every query.
  • Not a generic dashboard reskinned at the group level. The entity-and-ownership graph and the group roll-up are the center of the surface, not an afterthought.

02

The customer construct: how the cockpit assembles the group

Centerpiece

"Who is the customer" has two co-resident answers in a modern commercial bank. The cockpit reconciles both into one group, anchored on the subject account and resolved by a per-tenant groupingKey config.

This assembly is the heart of the solution: it is what makes the cockpit a relationship cockpit rather than an account viewer. nCino assembles the group as a relationship graph plus a per-facility involvement set plus an obligor roll-up. FSC assembles the same notion differently: a Group / Household is an Account, with members and financial accounts rolling up to it.

KYC is a customer / group property

Because the group is assembled once, KYC is a property of the customer, not the borrower: beneficial ownership across the group (Connection: Beneficial Owner / Owner %), CDD on the principals, and sanctions screening on every party in the group, not just the named borrower.

The household, two ways

The household / connections construct is central, and it is expressed differently in each model. In nCino the household is a set of edges in the Connection graph (Household, Household Member, Spouse roles) tying the operating company to its principals and their family. In FSC the household is a record: a Household Account that groups members via AccountContactRelation and related accounts via AccountAccountRelation, with financial accounts and assets / liabilities rolling up to it. The cockpit reconciles the two so a household expressed either way resolves to one group.

Why everything rolls up at group grain

Exposure (TBE / TOE), wallet (deposits + treasury unioned across nCino and FSC), risk (the binding covenant across facilities), KYC (every party screened), and profitability all aggregate at the obligor / household grain. The banker decides about the customer; the deal is the drill target underneath. This is why the obligor rollup, not the naive sum of facility limits, is the one true exposure number.

Illustrative examples (references, not the running subject)

Illustrative

The cockpit is generic. It assembles and concludes for any relationship group, of any node count, in either or both data models. The two examples below are clearly labeled illustrations of the construct at two ends of the spectrum.

Illustrative example · single-entity C&I obligor

Piedmont Precision illustrative. A C&I operating company. The simplest legitimate group: an operating-company borrower plus one 100% individual owner who is also the full-note guarantor. Two nodes.

The Customer 360 concludes concentration: the entire credit rests on one principal; owner equals guarantor equals a single point of both control and recourse, a credit positive (the owner is fully on the hook) and a concentration negative (key-person risk is total). The group has credit but no deposit or treasury relationship, so the headline move is the wallet gap: a credit-rich group with a hollow operating wallet is the largest unclaimed wallet on a performing name. Illustrates ownership + guaranty + obligor exposure with no deposit / household depth.

Illustrative example · FSC household

Timothy Norton Household illustrative. An FSC Household Account, $910,800. A Household Account grouping a primary member, a member, and a related operating business, with financial accounts rolling up to the group.

The Customer 360 concludes the inverse of the single-entity case: distribution, relationship value split across a person and a company under one household roof. Illustrates the people, the household, and the share-of-wallet roll-up at the group grain that a credit-only group structurally cannot show. The verdict lives on the wallet side: a relationship the bank holds, deposit-led, thin on treasury and lending, retain-then-deepen with the related business as the cross-sell.

The point of pairing them

The same generic cockpit, on the same group construct, concludes concentration for one customer and distribution for another, and leads with the credit verdict for one and the wallet verdict for the other, by reading the group, not by being coded for either. Three surfaces on one Product Package spine

Architecture

The brain is three surfaces co-existing on one nCino Product Package plus Account spine, with Boom as the shared spreading system of record and the same Boom to IRIS to nCino lineage running underneath all three.

The surfaces are not a stack where one sits beneath another. The Live Portfolio Dashboard is the book-level entry; the Customer 360 (the relationship cockpit) and the Credit Memo (the deal-level underwriting workspace) co-exist as co-equal working surfaces over the same Product Package plus Account spine, and they cross-reference each other. The 360 zooms out to the whole group; the memo zooms into one deal's underwriting; neither contains the other. The deal workspace is a peer surface a banker works in directly, not only a drill-in.

The functional spine: the 6-stage commercial-lending blueprint

Lifecycle · 1 of 2

The cockpit's spine is the recognized lifecycle of commercial lending, from first lead to a live, monitored facility, and it concludes at the customer / group level at each stage. A commercial banker does not think in sections; they think in where the customer sits between a first lead and a live, monitored facility, and what their job is at that point. Origination is the left half (01 to 04, win and underwrite the customer); maintenance and monitoring is the right half (05 to 06, board and watch the customer).

Three concerns run the full length of the relationship rather than living in any one stage: KYC, collateral, and covenant. Each is a first-class through-line the cockpit carries from origination into monitoring, concluding at the group grain at every step.

08

The Customer 360 surface: one relationship group

Cockpit · 1 of 3

The Customer 360 centers on the group entity-and-ownership graph and the group roll-up, not the deal. Everything below the header aggregates at the group / obligor grain. The deal is a drill-in.

7.1The group header and verdict

One banker-grade sentence with the watch item and the next-best-action baked in, asserted about the customer / group: the group rating, the whole obligor exposure (trust the group rollup, not the sum of facility tiles), the single watch item, and the headline move. The numbers inside the sentence are deterministic and locked; the prose lens is editable. Beneath it sits a thin evidence rail where each claim is a door that drills one provenance level down toward the system of record. Profitability is honest about absence: empty is not zero, it is "unmeasured." A group with credit exposure but no deposit, treasury, or fee relationship is "we earn spread on the drawn credit and capture none of the operating economics," and that absence is itself the conclusion that points at the wallet lever.

7.2Group sections (the depth under the stages)

Each section is one contract (Question / Conclusion / Evidence and drill path / Actions / Per-persona variation / Data anchors) and concludes at the group grain:

  • Entity and ownership graph (02). Who carries the risk across the group, who backs it, where the concentration sits, what changed. The diagram renders only when the group exceeds two nodes; below that, the sentence is the graph.
  • Exposure across the group plus collateral coverage (03 / 05 / 06). The one true group exposure number (committed / outstanding / available at the obligor rollup, never the naive sum of facility limits), where risk concentrates, whether lendable collateral covers it.
  • KYC and compliance (02 / 03 / 06). Beneficial ownership and clearance posture across every party in the group, concluding "cleared" or "unverified, blocks decisioning," never a fabricated clearance.
  • Financial spread, Boom snapshot (03). Whether the financials hold the grade and what is moving. The watch item is born here. Deterministic Boom engines compute every ratio; the model narrates.
  • Risk plus covenant compliance (03 / 04 / 06). Whether the credit is holding or sliding, room before a covenant trips, whether the grade defends itself. Thinnest cushion on normalized headroom; the rest collapse to one line.
  • Deposits / treasury plus share-of-wallet (supporting, all stages). Whether the bank owns the customer's money or just their risk, rolled up across the group; for a credit-rich group with a hollow wallet, the largest unmonetized opportunity in the relationship.
  • Whitespace and next-best-action plus alerts / EWS (01 / 06). The upside and the downside ranked together for the whole group, because a banker does not separate protect-this from grow-this.

Early Warning: triggers and how they are handled

EWS · 1 of 3

The Early Warning System is the cockpit's protect-the-book reflex. It detects, grades, ranks, and routes; a human classifies and decides. Every trigger is a deterministic server-side computation over verified data, a Boom ratio, a deal_covenant_grade, an AFS utilization read, an IRIS PD; the number never passes through the LLM. The model ranks the fired signals and narrates the watch story. Crossing a threshold raises a flag; it never moves a grade. Severity is a decision-support score, captured proposed-not-committed; the regulatory classification (Pass / Watch / Special Mention / Substandard / Doubtful / Loss) is the credit officer's act.

Every signal keys on the one Product Package + Account spine, so a fired trigger rolls up to the obligor / relationship group, never an isolated loan tile. An external trigger rarely fires alone; it raises the severity of a co-firing internal signal (idiosyncratic stress inside a stressed sector is worse than either). A single soft spread is noise; the trigger demands a sustained trend or a band crossing.

The EWS fence (SR 11-7, restated)

No EWS output writes a grade, a classification, or a regulated number. The cockpit fires triggers from deterministic compute, scores severity, proposes watch-list placement, raises the cadence, and drafts the watch narrative. The human confirms the signal is real, sets the watch grade, makes the regulatory classification, and owns the action plan. Every severity score and placement recommendation is captured proposed-not-committed, logged via log_audit_event, and becomes real only when the authorized human commits it via record_decision (classification authority enforced server-side against the acting user).

Hand in hand: where the information flows from

Data flow · 1 of 3

The Customer 360 is not a database with a chat box on top. It is the reading end of a fleet of MCP servers, each already the system of record for one slice of the customer, wired together so the cockpit can conclude across all of them without ever asking the model to do arithmetic. The principle that holds the fleet together governs everything else here: every regulated figure is produced by deterministic server-side code reading a verified field, and the model only ranks and narrates what that code returns. The fleet is how the cockpit earns the word "360"; the orchestration below is how it stays inside SR 11-7.

There are two kinds of source, and the difference is the whole orchestration story. nCino and FSC are inside Salesforce, one org, one identity. The SF-native Customer 360 MCP reads them directly, run-as-user, so the platform's record sharing and FLS are the floor on every query. Boom, AFS, IRIS, and the peer libraries are outside Salesforce. Those are reached through experience-mcp, the aggregation and governance tier that joins them server-side, keeps the numbers off the LLM path exactly as experience-mcp/boomFinancials.js already does for the built credit memo, and writes the decision ledger and audit trail. The cockpit never holds a connection string to Boom or IRIS; it asks experience-mcp for a conclusion and gets back a figure plus its lineage.

The two readers, and why they are different

The SF-native Customer 360 MCP reads nCino and FSC as the authenticated user, a deliberate upgrade over the credit memo, whose sfClient.js runs on a single service account and threads actingUser only as an audit stamp on writes. That is correct for one deal but wrong for a relationship cockpit reading far more of the customer, so the Customer 360 takes per-user OAuth (the sobject-sf shape) and the platform enforces sharing and FLS natively on every read. The single exception is the gated, audited admin god-mode, which widens visibility only, never write authority. The off-Salesforce sources go through experience-mcp because the join must happen where a regulated number is computed deterministically and never round-trips through the model: that tier pulls the Boom spread, AFS record, and IRIS rating, reconciles them server-side, attaches the lineage badge, and hands the cockpit a locked, sourced conclusion to narrate.

The spine every source keys on

All of it lands on one anchor: the nCino Product Package (LLC_BI__Product_Package__c) joined to its Account. The package carries the deterministic exposure rollups the cockpit trusts over any naive loan sum (TCE__c, TBE__c, TOE__c, Outstanding__c), netted across the limit/sublimit structure and honoring the exposure-exclusion flags. The relationship group is assembled off the same Account spine through the Connection graph and the FSC household overlay. The deal-level credit memo is not a separate system and not a layer beneath the 360; it is a co-resident peer surface and a source on that same package (nCino cm_* fields, LLC_BI__Credit_Memo__c), co-existing with the 360 and feeding it the rating, grades, narratives and decisions. One package, one Account, surfaces that feed each other.

The Boom → IRIS → nCino lineage

The regulated grade has a fixed lineage, and the cockpit walks it forward as navigation, not a footnote. Boom spreads the raw statement and computes the ratios; IRIS on Snowflake consumes the spread to produce the PD and the risk rating; nCino carries the rating of record (LLC_BI__Risk_Rating__c) and the covenant grade (deal_covenant_grade). Drilling the grade descends that exact chain: nCino rating, to the IRIS PD, to the Boom line items underneath. The drill path is the audit trail walked forward by a banker.

Borrower Health Index (BHI): two numbers, by design

The nCino risk grade (LLC_BI__Risk_Rating__c) stays the regulatory anchor, the official classification a human owns. Alongside it, a deterministic 0-100 Borrower Health Index is the early-warning lens: higher = healthier (the inverse of severity), computed server-side in code, never through the LLM (SR 11-7). Inputs are the seven EWS signal classes, each scored 0-3 (financial / covenant / servicing / risk-migration / deposit / external / structural). Banded (illustrative defaults, config): 80-100 Healthy · 60-79 Watch · 40-59 Elevated · <40 Critical.

It is decision-support, not a grade: a band change raises a flag, it never moves the grade, and it never overrides the regulatory classification. No black box, every BHI shows its contributing signals and the source field behind each (drill to the Boom ratio, the covenant cushion, the AFS utilization). Default weights ship today; the bank's incoming EWS scorecard weights replace them by config, no rework.

Self-learning, memory, and governance

Brain layer

These behaviors are wired into the surface, not bolted on, and they all respect the conclude-not-compute boundary.

Self-learning that ranks, never computes. The cockpit gets sharper per customer and across the book by tuning which conclusions surface, in what order, framed how. No learned signal is ever an input to a regulated figure. The brain learns which watch items mattered, not what the DSC cushion is.
Memory: decision ledger plus audit plus recall. The cockpit remembers why the bank did what it did (the editable decision ledger, record_decision), what was done (the append-only audit trail, log_audit_event / get_audit_trail), and warms the next analyst who walks into the customer (recall_decisions). Memory informs judgment; the regulated numbers are re-derived live on every open and never read back from the ledger.
SR 11-7 decision-support. The whole pipeline, drafting and review alike, is decision-support, never an autonomous credit decision. Effective challenge, full data lineage (Boom to IRIS to nCino), human-in-the-loop attestation, DRAFT-until-committee, an auditable decision ledger, and separation of development (the drafting agent) from validation (the reviewer agent) are concrete behaviors.
Run-as-user security. Per-user OAuth so Salesforce sharing and FLS are the platform-enforced floor on every query at the group grain, including FLS-before-aggregation and the partial-view marker ("N related entities in this group not visible to you"). The single intentional exception is a gated, audited admin god-mode that widens visibility only, never write authority or a delegated limit, with a persistent elevated-context banner.

The decision ledger and audit, on Snowflake

What the bank concluded and why does not live in the model's context; it lives in experience-mcp's memory layer, backed on Snowflake alongside the IRIS data. The editable decision ledger (record_decision) holds the why. The append-only audit trail (log_audit_event / get_audit_trail) holds the what, with a server-set actor and timestamp the agent cannot override, and with AI-proposed cleanly separated from human-attested. Recall (recall_decisions) warms the next analyst who opens the customer with what prior cycles concluded. Memory informs judgment; it is never the source of a regulated figure. Every regulated number is re-derived live on every open, off the deterministic sources, and never read back from the ledger. That, together with the Boom → IRIS → nCino lineage, is the artifact that survives an examiner.

The editable boundary

A banker may change how a number is framed, record what they conclude about it, and write the story around it. They may never change the number. The boundary is enforced server-side, not by hiding a button.
