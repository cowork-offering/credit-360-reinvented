# Credit 360 Cockpit — Commercial Banking Persona Reference

This reference grounds the seven Customer 360 personas in how the job actually works in a US commercial / C&I bank. It is the relationship-level companion to the deal-level credit memo: the cockpit concludes from the whole data fleet (nCino + FSC + Boom spreads + covenant/risk analytics) and lets each role drill and act inside their lane. Personas map to PERSONAS.md 4.1 through 4.7.

---

## 1. Per-Role Profiles

### 4.1 Relationship Manager / Commercial Loan Officer
**Who they are.** Front-line owner of a book of C&I borrowers (roughly $5M to $250M facilities). The "producer who also manages": originate, structure, recommend, monitor, retain, grow. Owns Prospecting and Sales stages; feeds Servicing & Monitoring.
**Owns.** Pipeline prioritization; proposed structure, facility sizing, collateral/guaranty ask, indicative pricing on the term sheet; the risk-rating recommendation and credit narrative to committee; pricing/fee trade-offs within delegated limits; the call to flag a deteriorating credit; the front-door KYC/CIP evidence. Does NOT own final approval, the assigned rating, the binding covenant package, policy-exception grants, or SAR filing.
**Measured on.** Loan production and outstandings vs. goal; deposit and treasury revenue (now near-equal weight to loans); fee income; net-new and retained relationships; relationship profitability / RAROC; portfolio credit quality (criticized, non-accrual, past-due, charge-offs in the book); annual-review/renewal timeliness; cross-sell depth; pipeline pull-through. Base ~$100K to 110K, total often >$150K.
**Top system needs.** One connected relationship view (entities, guarantors, total exposure, deposits/treasury wallet, risk, profitability) assembled without re-stitching; CORRECT total exposure from nCino Package roll-ups (never naive-summed); "what is my book" ranked by attention/whitespace/renewal-due; whitespace and next-best-action surfaced; deal status plus open questions and prior rationale; covenant and EWS alerts; treasury signals; read-only on sourced figures with provenance plus relationship notes.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair across nCino, core/AFS, spreading tool, CRM, DocMan, email; manual re-keying so logging rots; no single source of truth for total exposure (double-counting); stale, fragmented wallet/profitability data; admin eating calling time; reactive risk found late; re-explaining the relationship every renewal; chasing financials/BBCs.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** KYC/CIP/CDD and beneficial ownership at the front door; BSA/AML escalation; OFAC screening of borrower/guarantors/principals; fair lending / ECOA Reg B and emerging 1071; credit-policy adherence and exception documentation; safety-and-soundness (timely reviews, current financials); FIRREA appraisal rules; exam-ready files; Reg O awareness.

### 4.2 Commercial Credit Portfolio Manager
**Who they are.** The book-health steward between the RM (revenue) and the Credit Officer (approval). The credit-quality conscience: keeps ratings accurate, covenants tested, reviews current, deterioration surfaced early. Owns Servicing & Monitoring. Lending-side, not markets-side.
**Owns.** The proposed/recommended risk rating; whether a name belongs on the watch list and its action plan; recommended structure on renewals/modifications; whether a covenant breach is technical-curable vs. real deterioration; monitoring timing and depth; identification of policy exceptions. Within a personal authority may approve smaller credits. Does NOT own final approval above authority, credit policy, the rating scale, pricing grids, the ACL/CECL methodology, the customer relationship, or moving a credit to Special Assets unilaterally.
**Measured on.** Annual-review timeliness (near-zero overdue); document/financial-statement exception rate and aging; rating accuracy vs. loan review; asset quality (past-due, non-accrual, criticized/classified, charge-offs); favorable vs. adverse grade migration; covenant-test coverage on time; watch-list management; portfolio capacity; renewal cycle time; audit/exam results. Incentives skew to quality and timeliness, NOT loan growth (deliberate independence).
**Top system needs.** Single relationship 360 with trusted total-exposure roll-up; a live covenant panel (threshold, actual, cushion %, status, next test) recomputed on new financials; trended spreads with deterioration auto-flagged and peer/NAICS context; a risk-rating workspace where the PM can PROPOSE (not silently rewrite); a ranked early-warning surface; a review/renewal/maturity work queue with ticklers; concentration roll-ups across the book; exception tracking with aging; fast recall of prior decisions; servicing signals joined to the credit view; a drill path book → 360 → facility/covenant.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair with no SSoT; calendar-driven monitoring surfacing breaches months late; manual re-spreading and re-keying; document chasing; no portfolio-wide covenant/grade view; exposure double-counting; annual-review backlog and exam fear; EWS signals living apart from the credit file; manual Excel reporting; context loss between people; too much file mechanics, too little judgment.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** OCC Loan Portfolio Management; OCC Rating Credit Risk (Pass/SM/Substandard/Doubtful/Loss, annual + event-driven); Interagency Credit Risk Review (SR 13-1); ACL/CECL (ASC 326) dependency on rating accuracy; SR 11-7 (human-in-the-loop); lending-authority and exception framework; appraisal/collateral currency; CRE concentration guidance; BSA/AML/OFAC/fair-lending surfacing (PII gated); exam readiness on review timeliness and rating accuracy.

### 4.3 Commercial Credit Analyst / Underwriter
**Who they are.** The development side of underwriting: spreads the financials, builds global cash flow, writes the memo, proposes the ratings and structure. Owns the Boom spread in the cockpit.
**Owns.** How to normalize/spread and which add-backs/adjustments to apply (documented); the analytical conclusions, repayment-source hierarchy, DSCR/leverage/coverage, and proposed borrower (PD) + facility (LGD) ratings; recommended structure, covenant package, monitoring triggers, and the approve/approve-with-conditions/decline recommendation; surfacing and quantifying exceptions. Does NOT decide final approval, the committed rating, final pricing/terms, exception grants, or post-close waivers/modifications.
**Measured on.** Turnaround / cycle time (the headline throughput metric); deals-and-renewals per analyst and officers-supported ratio; credit-file quality (spreads tie to source, clean exception docs, low rework); rating accuracy and stability (low override, low subsequent downgrade); lagging portfolio outcomes on credits underwritten; annual-review/renewal timeliness. Typically NOT on production commission (independent judgment).
**Top system needs.** One assembled relationship spine with trusted total exposure; a spread + ratio surface with full lineage (provenance-locked, adjustments layered as records not in-place edits); covenant-actual vs. nCino-threshold at a glance with thinnest cushion flagged; a repayment-source / global-cash-flow view assembled without re-keying; decision memory (prior analyst + committee rationale); an assigned-deals queue (own book, least-privilege) ranked by underwriting priority.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair across spreading tool, LOS/nCino, core, DocMan, bureaus, Excel (assembling from 4+ systems can mean ~1,300 hours/year); manual re-keying from PDFs/tax returns (biggest time-sink, tie-out errors); stale/fragmented data; no single relationship view and naive double-counting; re-explaining context every cycle; chasing documents and BBCs; the speed-vs-defensibility squeeze.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** Credit policy and OCC underwriting expectations; SR 11-7 (output is decision-support, development separated from validation); regulatory rating definitions; BSA/AML and KYC/CIP reliance; OFAC screening of borrower/guarantors/related parties; fair lending / ECOA Reg B (applies to commercial, adverse-action handling); appraisal/collateral and FIRREA independence (12 CFR 34); exam, credit-review, and SNC readiness with full data lineage.

### 4.4 Credit Officer / Senior & Chief Credit Officer (Approver)
**Who they are.** The validation-side decision authority. Performs effective challenge on a drafted, attested package and holds approve/decline/modify authority up to a delegated limit; above it, routes to committee. The CCO also owns the credit-risk framework. This is the Approver persona (4.4), NOT the read-only Executive.
**Owns.** The credit decision within authority (approve / approve-with-conditions / modify / decline); the final regulatory risk grade and any override of the analyst's grade; classification and adverse-action calls (downgrade, watch-list, non-accrual, TDR, charge-off recommendation); policy-exception decisions; conditions of approval; whether to sponsor up to committee. Does NOT own who the customer is or commercial pricing, the spread/memo narrative, the regulated number computation, enterprise risk appetite above board bounds, the operational booking, or unilateral decisions above its limit.
**Measured on.** Asset-quality ratios (NCO, NPL/NPA, past-due, classified-to-capital-plus-allowance); regulator/loan-review adverse rating changes; allowance adequacy/coverage; portfolio yield and pre-tax income contribution (quality balanced against profitable growth); concentration adherence; decision turnaround/SLA; exception volume and aging; exam/audit outcomes (MRAs avoided/remediated).
**Top system needs.** An approval queue landing the fully assembled, attested package in one place; a single relationship 360 with trusted exposure roll-up, guarantor graph, deposits/treasury, risk; the decision evidence chain (deterministic covenant grade, ratio actuals with lineage, prior decision + audit trail); explicit authority context (required level vs. acting officer's limit, clear route-to-committee); exception and concentration visibility at decision time; early-warning and migration signals; a write surface scoped to the decision act only; an examiner-ready immutable audit trail distinguishing AI-proposed from human-attested.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair to reconstruct the relationship for challenge; no single trustworthy exposure view (double-counting); stale/re-keyed data; effective challenge unsupported (rationale buried in PDFs/email); approval-queue overload and slow cycle time; exceptions/concentrations tracked in side spreadsheets so limit breaches are invisible at decision time; weak audit trail and hard-to-prove rating independence.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** Credit-risk-review independence (SR 13-1); regulatory classifications (FDIC RMS §3.2, OCC Rating Credit Risk); ACL/ALLL under CECL; legal lending limits (12 CFR 32) and concentration/CRE guidance; ECOA/Reg B adverse-action obligations; BSA/AML and OFAC; appraisal/FIRREA; SR 11-7 (human effective challenge); Reg O, HMDA/Reg C, flood/Reg H where applicable; exam readiness as the credit-side examiner interface.

### 4.5 Commercial Loan Operations / Servicing Officer
**Who they are.** Owns post-approval execution and lifecycle servicing: loan boarding to the core, then advances, payments, payoffs, renewals/mods, covenant/financial-statement ticklers, collateral/insurance/UCC perfection, exception clearing. Owns Offering & Set-up and servicing handoff. Decision-support consumer: read-only on sourced figures, no rating/approval authority.
**Owns.** Whether booked terms faithfully match the approved package, and refusing to fund until docs/conditions are satisfied (the operational funding gate); whether a covenant/document/collateral exception is cleared/pending/escalated and its materiality routing; collateral-perfection actions (UCC continuation, force-placement trigger, lien release); operational accuracy of maintenance transactions and GL-to-core reconciliation. Does NOT decide the credit, rating, approval, pricing, structure, covenant thresholds, waivers/modifications, or the charge-off determination.
**Measured on.** Loan-boarding accuracy / error rate (headline); boarding/funding turnaround vs. SLA; documentation exception rate and aging (past 30/60/90); covenant/financial-statement tickler currency; collateral perfection currency (UCC lapse, insurance lapse, FDPA 45-day timeliness); past-due processing and notice accuracy; straight-through-processing rate and volume per FTE; GL-to-core reconciliation timeliness and exam findings.
**Top system needs.** A boarding worksheet putting approved terms side-by-side with what is about to be keyed, with a reconcile/diff before funding; a single facility/limit/sublimit view from the approved nCino Package (never naive-summed); a live exception and tickler worklist ranked by aging and regulatory clock; one-click servicing-handoff actions (reserve obligation number, create workpackage) with completion state; read-only, always-current, provenance-locked sourced figures; collateral/documentation footprint per relationship; an audit-ready append-only trail; pushed alerts for covenant tests, insurance/UCC expiry, overdue financials.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair across LOS, core (FIS/AFS), imaging, UCC service, insurance tracker, Excel ticklers; manual re-keying of approved terms at boarding (largest error source); weak tickler/exception tracking aging into findings; booked record drifting from the approved package; post-closing cleanup churn; no relationship-level context (loan seen in isolation); reactive audit-driven discovery; high-volume deadline-bound work with manual controls.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** FDPA flood insurance, force-placement and 45-day notice; UCC Article 9 perfection (filings, continuations, terminations); OCC/NCUA loan-administration exception expectations; hazard/tax-escrow administration; BSA/AML and OFAC servicing-side touchpoints (screening new parties at boarding, monitoring payment activity); accuracy of statements/payoffs/interest; exam and audit readiness (SOX-style maintenance controls at larger banks); SR 11-7 posture (decision-support, provenance-locked, no autonomous credit action).

### 4.6 Treasury Management / Cash Management Officer
**Who they are.** Revenue-carrying, fee-and-deposit specialist who owns the non-credit operating side. A functional overlay on the RM: grows commercial deposit balances and treasury-services fee income across a book. Operating-relationship and share-of-wallet owner; read-only on credit, write only on opportunity/cross-sell notes.
**Owns.** TM solution design (which products to bundle and the cross-sell sequence); pricing/structure within delegated authority (per-service fees, ECR, hard-dollar vs. balance mix, exception-pricing justification); deal economics and prioritization (which credit-rich/deposit-poor relationships to attack first); proposal content and negotiation posture; retention plays. Does NOT own the credit decision/rating/structure/loan pricing, the final KYC/CDD determination or SAR filing, pricing beyond delegated limits, or account opening over a CIP/OFAC hit.
**Measured on.** TM fee (non-interest) revenue (headline, new + total + YoY); operating deposit growth (low-cost balances weighted, deposit beta quality); new-business production and products-per-relationship; win/conversion and pipeline coverage; retention/attrition; activity (calls, QBRs, RFPs); implementation effectiveness (time-to-revenue, clean go-live). Comp base + incentive, US average total ~$147K.
**Top system needs.** A single share-of-wallet view per relationship (deposits + TM services held vs. potential, whitespace map of unused services); a credit-rich / deposit-poor targeting queue ranked by opportunity and timing (loan closing imminent = capture now); net treasury/deposit revenue per relationship; read-only credit context (exposure, covenants, grade, deal status) to time the approach; trigger alerts (loan booked/closing, balance run-off, competitor takeaway, renewal/QBR, fee drop); one-place workflow capture with clean implementation hand-off.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair across loan system, DDA core, separate TM origination/onboarding platform, account-analysis/billing, CRM; no out-of-box share-of-wallet view (whitespace inferred manually, opportunities never seen); cross-sell timing missed (the credit trigger lives in the RM's pipeline); sold-but-not-implemented gap and slow time-to-revenue; stale deposit/fee data (industry tracks deposits but rarely TM sales activity without spreadsheets); manual proposal/account-analysis grunt work; fragmented credit context; weak attribution/pipeline hygiene.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** KYC/CIP/CDD and beneficial-ownership collection at account opening (feeds BSA/Compliance); BSA/AML monitoring awareness and escalation, CTR awareness; OFAC and PEP screening before services go live; payments-rules compliance baked into services sold (NACHA / Reg GG for ACH, UCC 4A for wires, Reg CC for availability/RDC, Reg D legacy awareness, Reg E scoping); TM master agreement plus addenda and DACAs where deposits are pledged; transparent fee disclosure and accurate account-analysis; FDIC insurance / public-funds collateralization; exam readiness on onboarding and screening records.

### 4.7 Portfolio / Credit-Risk Executive (CRO / CCO oversight slice)
**Who they are.** Top-of-house owner of credit-risk strategy and portfolio quality across the commercial book. In the cockpit this is the CEO/COO read-only oversight persona (no per-deal approval): portfolio quality, concentration, risk migration, ACL adequacy, exam readiness. (A CCO's per-deal approval authority maps to 4.4.)
**Owns.** The credit-policy and risk-appetite framework (concentration limits, hold limits, rating scale, downgrade triggers, exception thresholds, delegated authorities); portfolio-level risk calls (cap/trim a concentration, tighten a sector, move a name to watch/special assets, charge-off posture); ACL adequacy sign-off and Q-factor judgment; the credit story to board and examiners. CCO-level also owns final decisions above committee authority. Co-owns risk appetite and capital with CFO/CEO/board. Does NOT decide individual deal approvals in the oversight slice, single-deal pricing, the ratio math, or the GAAP ACL number alone.
**Measured on.** Asset-quality ratios (NPL/non-accrual, classified/criticized, classified-to-capital, past-due) with migration; NCO rate and provision; ACL coverage and adequacy; rating migration (net downgrades, watch-list growth, weighted-average grade drift); concentration vs. limit as % of Tier 1 + ACL and breach severity; RAROC and yield vs. expected loss; exception volume/aging, overdue reviews, stale ratings; exam/audit outcomes (MRAs, time-to-remediate, SNC pass-through); growth-vs-quality balance.
**Top system needs.** A single book-level view rolling up total commercial exposure (committed/outstanding, funded/unfunded) sliceable by industry/NAICS, geography, grade band, product, officer, single-borrower group, trusting nCino roll-ups; a live concentration dashboard with each bucket vs. limit and vs. Tier 1 + ACL with proximity/breach flags; risk-migration and watch-list analytics plus an aggregated deterioration EWS feed; drill-down portfolio → cohort → single-customer 360 → deal memo; exam/audit-ready provenance (Boom → Snowflake → nCino) and append-only trail; fast answers to standing questions; pushed alerting on breaches, new classifieds, overdue reviews, stale ratings.
**Top pain points.** Swivel-chair with no SSoT (comprehensive view assembled by hand); manual lagging board/committee Excel reporting (stale by the time it is final); data fragmentation hiding cross-signals (e.g. office-CRE vacancy meeting a CRE concentration); naive double-counting and reconciliation distrust; no real-time concentration-vs-capital view; stale ratings and overdue reviews unflagged; weeks-long exam prep because provenance is not data; broken drill-down from aggregate to evidence.
**Regulatory touchpoints.** OCC Concentrations of Credit (Bulletin 2020-90, vs. Tier 1 + ACL); OCC Rating Credit Risk; ACL/CECL governance (OCC ACL handbook, ASC 326, SR 11-7 over the CECL model); Interagency Credit Risk Review (SR 13-1); Shared National Credit program; leveraged-lending (SR 13-3) and CRE concentration guidance (100%/300% criteria); board/risk-appetite governance (SR 16-11, Heightened Standards); BSA/AML, OFAC, KYC, fair-lending (ECOA/Reg B, CRA), FIRREA, and CAMELS asset-quality exam readiness.

## 2. What the Cockpit Must Do for Each Role

### 4.1 Relationship Manager
- **Conclude the relationship in one view.** One connected single-customer 360 (entities, guarantors, total exposure from trusted Package roll-ups, deposits/treasury wallet, risk, profitability) so the RM walks in prepared without swivel-chairing.
- **Lead with whitespace as next-best-action.** For a credit-rich / deposit-poor borrower (the Piedmont $0-wallet pattern), conclude "win the operating deposits + treasury" and list the specific unused services. Cross-sell is the headline, not a buried tab.
- **Push early-warning and review-due alerts.** Thinnest covenant cushion, utilization spikes, slow pay, past-due financials/BBCs, annual reviews coming due, surfaced as a prioritized "who needs attention" queue on the book.
- **Recall the deal, do not re-explain it.** Open a borrower already knowing deal status, open questions, and prior decisions + rationale, cross-linked into the deal-level credit memo. Keep the RM read-only on sourced figures with relationship notes only (separation of duties).

### 4.2 Portfolio Manager
- **Conclude book health, do not just display it.** Rank relationships by a deterioration/attention score fusing covenant cushion, grade trend, utilization spikes, payment slippage, and stale-financials age, so the riskiest names rise to the top.
- **Surface a live covenant + headroom verdict per relationship.** Deterministically computed server-side (Boom ratios × nCino thresholds, never via the LLM), flagging the thinnest cushion and anything trending toward breach BEFORE the test date, so breaches are caught in-quarter, not at next annual review.
- **Drive the rating + review workflow as propose-not-commit.** Show current grade, trend, the driving ratios/factors, and a committee-ready proposed grade; let the PM propose downgrade / add-to-watch / trigger-interim-review, attributable and logged, with the actual write staying with the Credit Officer.
- **Make the review/renewal/exception backlog a first-class queue.** Ticklers and aging (overdue reviews, upcoming maturities, missing financials/certs, open exceptions), with a jump from any relationship into the deal memo.

### 4.3 Credit Analyst
- **Conclude total relationship exposure correctly.** From nCino Package roll-ups (honoring exposure-exclusion flags, never naive-summing limit/sublimit), so the analyst underwrites against a trustworthy single number.
- **Surface every covenant actual vs. threshold deterministically**, with pass/watch/breach and thinnest cushion flagged, and let the analyst drill from any breach into the driving ratio and its lineage.
- **Let adjustments be first-class records, not edits.** Adjusted EBITDA, add-backs, pro-forma layered on top of provenance-locked sourced values; where an adjustment moves a pass/watch/breach grade, flag it with dual lineage for the officer's effective challenge.
- **Recall prior decisions and feed the memo.** Start each renewal with context, not a blank page; feed the relationship 360 directly into the deal-level memo. Keep read-only on sourced figures while fully enabling narrative authoring (development-vs-validation separation, SR 11-7).

### 4.4 Credit Officer / Approver
- **Surface a decision-ready package.** Lay the deterministic covenant grade, ratio actuals (with lineage), full exposure roll-up, and the analyst's attested rationale side-by-side, and flag any analyst adjustment that moved a grade as a point to challenge, so the officer challenges, does not reassemble.
- **Make authority and routing explicit.** Conclude this package's required approval level vs. the acting officer's delegated limit and surface an unambiguous approve-within-authority vs. route-to-committee state, enforced server-side against the acting user.
- **Scope the write surface tightly.** The only writes are the approval transition (approve/reject) and decision rationale/attestation; never edit a sourced figure; every action self-fires an immutable, examiner-ready audit entry distinguishing AI-proposed from human-attested.
- **Conclude limit and concentration impact at decision time.** Show whether approving breaches single-borrower / industry / CRE concentration or carries unresolved exceptions with mitigants, plus risk-in-the-round and early warning, so the call reflects trajectory, not just the snapshot.

### 4.5 Loan Operations / Servicing
- **Conclude a boarding-readiness verdict.** Reconcile approved-package terms vs. what will be (or was) booked to the core, flag every mismatch, and gate the servicing-handoff actions (reserve obligation number, create workpackage) on documentation/conditions being satisfied.
- **Surface a single, aging-ranked exception and tickler queue as the home view.** Open doc exceptions, UCC continuations due, flood/hazard insurance lapsing, overdue covenant/financial-statement items, each with the regulatory clock (e.g. FDPA 45-day window) visible.
- **Give relationship-in-the-round context, read-only and provenance-locked**, so reconciliation and post-closing cleanup happen against one trusted view, while keeping whitespace/credit-judgment surfaces hidden per scope.
- **Enforce the decision boundary as a control.** Ops authors documentation/checklist notes and fires the gated AFS booking/handoff write tools (validated against the Loan Ops permission) but is rejected from any rating/approval/figure-edit action; every boarding and maintenance action is logged append-only for QC and exam.

### 4.6 Treasury / Cash Management Officer
- **Conclude and surface the share-of-wallet gap.** Render deposits + each TM service held vs. the full suite, and headline the missing services and the $0/low-wallet flag (the Piedmont pattern) as the next-best-action, from a server-side wallet roll-up with config-driven treasury picklist.
- **Build the credit-rich / deposit-poor cross-sell queue automatically.** Filter the shared book to relationships where exposure exists or a loan is closing/renewing but the deposit/TM wallet is thin, ranked by opportunity size and credit-event timing.
- **Trigger the loan-closing / renewal cross-sell moment.** When a Product Package books, closes, or renews, alert the treasury overlay so the operating account is captured at the one moment it is easiest to win, closing the RM-pipeline-to-TMO visibility gap.
- **Show net treasury/deposit revenue with FLS-respecting provenance**, and let the TMO act in place: read-only credit context plus write-capable opportunity/cross-sell notes and a clean opportunity → proposal → implementation hand-off, never widening beyond the shared book.

### 4.7 Portfolio / Credit-Risk Executive
- **Conclude concentration risk at the top.** Each industry/NAICS, geography, single-borrower, and collateral bucket as a live % of Tier 1 + ACL with proximity-to-limit and breach flags, computed server-side, never naive-summed, honoring exposure-exclusion flags.
- **Conclude risk migration and early warning across the subordinate book.** Net downgrades, watch-list growth, new classifieds, covenant breaches, utilization spikes, and payment slippage rolled into a single deterioration view with grade-drift over time.
- **Make drill-down work end to end.** Portfolio aggregate → segment/cohort → single-customer 360 → deal memo, every number traceable with Boom → Snowflake → nCino lineage on each section.
- **Stay strictly decision-support and read-only.** No per-deal write or approval at this tier; all roll-ups server-side and provenance-locked; the view exactly as wide as Salesforce record sharing grants, with an append-only audit/decision trail that doubles as the exam-ready credit story.

## 3. Cross-Role Themes

### Shared needs (the cockpit's universal job)
1. **One single connected view.** Every role re-stitches the relationship by hand across nCino, core/AFS, spreading tool, CRM, DocMan, and email. All seven want the borrower, its entities/affiliates/guarantors, total exposure, deposits/treasury, risk, and profitability assembled once. The cockpit's reason to exist.
2. **Total exposure that is CORRECT.** Naive-summing limits/sublimits and affiliated entities double-counts. Every credit-facing role must trust the nCino Package roll-up (honoring exposure-exclusion flags) instead of rebuilding the number.
3. **Fresh, provenance-locked data.** Deposit, fee, covenant-actual, and rating data live in different systems with different refresh cadences. Every role wants current values with lineage (Boom → Snowflake → nCino), and every role except the two writers consumes them read-only.
4. **No swivel-chair.** The same complaint recurs verbatim across all seven: 4+ logins, manual re-keying, data that rots because logging steals selling/judgment time.
5. **Deterministic conclusions, not LLM math.** Covenant grades, exposure roll-ups, and concentration-vs-capital are computed server-side; the LLM narrates and recalls, it never computes a regulated figure. This is the SR 11-7 spine.
6. **Fast answers to "where does this stand and what do I do."** Each role's standing questions differ (whitespace for the RM, cushion for the PM, total exposure for the analyst, authority/concentration for the officer, boarding-readiness for ops, share-of-wallet for the TMO, concentration-vs-capital for the executive) but the shape is identical: conclude, then let me drill and act.
7. **Memory and recall.** Prior decisions and rationale must be retrievable so no banker re-explains the relationship to a blank page on renewal or hand-off.
8. **Pushed alerts, not pulled reports.** Covenant tests, utilization spikes, review-due, insurance/UCC expiry, balance run-off, limit breaches, surfaced as a prioritized queue rather than hunted across reports.

### Role tensions the cockpit must hold
- **RM growth vs. credit-risk discipline (4.1/4.6 vs. 4.2/4.4/4.7).** The RM and TMO are incentivized to grow loans, deposits, and fees; the PM, Credit Officer, and Executive are incentivized on quality and timeliness and deliberately NOT on production. The same 360 must lead with whitespace/cross-sell for the producers and with deterioration/cushion/concentration for the risk side. The cockpit resolves this by persona-scoped verdicts on shared data, not separate tools.
- **Analyst development vs. officer validation (4.3 vs. 4.4).** SR 11-7 requires development separated from validation. The analyst proposes ratings, layers adjustments as records, and authors the narrative; the officer challenges and commits. The cockpit enforces this as a control: analyst writes are narrative + adjustment records (never the committed figure); officer writes are the approval transition + attestation only.
- **PM proposes vs. officer commits (4.2 vs. 4.4).** The PM owns the recommended rating and watch-list call but not the committed grade. Propose-not-commit must be a first-class, attributable, logged state, not a blank in a field the PM can silently overwrite.
- **Ops execution gate vs. credit authority (4.5 vs. 4.4).** Ops can refuse to fund until conditions are met (an operational gate) but cannot waive a covenant or change a term. The boundary is a server-side permission check, not a UI convention.
- **Relationship breadth vs. record-sharing scope (4.6/4.7).** A wallet opportunity or a portfolio aggregate must never widen a user's view beyond what Salesforce sharing grants. A "partial view, N entities not visible to you" marker is the honest answer when affiliates are not shared.

## 4. Gaps in the Current Cockpit Design

Current sections (header/verdict, entity graph, exposure+collateral, deposits/treasury, risk+covenants, whitespace/NBA) and pillars (drill, edit, self-learning, memory, policy) cover the relationship-snapshot well. Gaps these real roles need:

1. **No time/work queue surface.** Five of seven roles live by ticklers and dates (annual reviews due, maturities, FDPA 45-day windows, UCC continuations, financial-statement due dates, QBRs). The current sections describe one relationship's state; they do not give a cross-book, aging-ranked "what is due / overdue across my portfolio" view. **Suggestion:** add a **Work Queue / Tickler** surface keyed off the same data, persona-filtered (PM = reviews + covenant tests; Ops = doc/UCC/insurance exceptions with regulatory clocks; RM = renewals + whitespace; Executive = overdue-review and stale-rating roll-up). This is the home screen for the PM, Ops, and arguably the RM.

2. **No portfolio / book-level roll-up tier.** Header/verdict and entity graph are single-customer. The PM works concentration across the book, and the Executive works concentration-vs-Tier-1+ACL, migration, and watch-list trend, which have no home in the current single-relationship sections. **Suggestion:** add a **Portfolio Roll-up** tier above the 360 with concentration-vs-limit, grade migration, and an aggregated deterioration feed, drilling down into the existing single-customer sections (the drill pillar extended upward, not just downward).

3. **No explicit authority / routing model.** The Credit Officer needs required-approval-level vs. acting-officer-limit and a route-to-committee state; Ops needs a fund/no-fund gate; the edit pillar today is generic. **Suggestion:** make **delegated-authority context a first-class element of header/verdict and the edit pillar**, computed server-side against the acting user, so approve-within-authority vs. escalate is a visible, enforced state, not a hidden menu.
